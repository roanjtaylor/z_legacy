## 1. Where I Am

**Who.** 21 years old. BEng Mechatronic Engineering at Ulster University Belfast. One year of the degree remaining — the placement/gap year was spent building the startup.

**What I'm doing.** Building a SaaS startup. Landed first paying customer — real validation, but too early to bet my life on. Treating it simultaneously as (a) a high-EV bet and (b) a deep apprenticeship in how the startup world actually works.

**Strengths.** Engineering background; I can ship. Already past one of the hardest filters: someone paid me money at 21.

**Constraints.**
- One year of degree to finish.

- No safety net of a giant trust fund — financial freedom has to be earned.

**North Star.** Build a *personal wealth engine* — enough recurring income or savings to be free to do what I want (travel, food & drink, learn deeply) — while doing meaningful work that creates real value for real people (Paul Graham's "make something people want" applied to a life, not just a startup).

## 2. The Real Question

> **What should I optimize for in the next 2–3 years to maximize the probability of reaching financial freedom + meaningful work by my early 30s — while keeping all three paths open as long as possible?**

The urgency is real. A close friend just landed a £150k grad role at Merricks Capital as a commodity trader at age 20, rotating Singapore/London/Mozambique/Brazil. That's the proof point: career compounding starts *now*, not at 25. I don't want to waste this window.

But Paul Graham's advice cuts the other way: *enjoy being young, explore widely, build character and worldview before family/responsibility narrow the lens.* This is the time to tip-toe down all paths.

The plan has to reconcile both: move fast enough to compound, but wide enough to discover.

## 3. The Three Paths

### Path A — Builder
**Tech job. High pay. Real engineering problems. Lower variance.**

- **What it is.** Senior IC role at a great tech company — Anthropic, Stripe, Figma, Linear, a deep-tech startup, etc.
- **What it gives me.** Salary (£100k–£300k+ trajectory), elite branding for life, network of strong engineers, real technical depth, savings/equity cushion.
- **What it costs.** Lower variance also means lower upside ceiling. Easy to get golden-handcuffed. Hard to leave when you're earning well.
- **Who I want to study.** Staff/principal engineers at frontier AI labs and infra companies; CTOs who built deep before founding.

### Path B — Hustler
**VC / investing / deal-driven. Learn capital, founders, markets.**

- **What it is.** Analyst/associate role at a VC firm, EF/accelerator, or a high-leverage BD/strategy seat.
- **What it gives me.** Investment fluency, founder network, pattern recognition for what wins and why, optionality to angel-invest or start a fund.
- **What it costs.** Harder to break into without prior signal (the irony). Slower path to large outcomes unless you become a partner. Less direct technical skill building.
- **Who I want to study.** Gary Davidson (NI — sales → founder → angel → fund); Ian Browne (NI — founder → exit → investor); analysts at Sequoia/Index/Atomico/EF; emerging-manager partners.

### Path C — Founder
**Build my own. Highest variance. Highest upside. Highest grind.**

- **What it is.** Ship and sell. Probably not the current startup — more likely a *second* attempt, with sharpened domain insight and a better cap table.
- **What it gives me.** Total ownership of outcome. Maximum learning rate. Asymmetric financial upside on exit.
- **What it costs.** Most outcomes are slow grinds or quiet failures. Survivorship bias is brutal — the visible "winners" are a tiny tail. VC-funded headline founders often look rich on paper for years without actually winning. The "made it at 21" archetype (Altman, Collisons) is *the dangerous one to pattern-match to*.
- **Who I want to study.** Max Junestrand (Legora — 23, stacked the right internships); James Hawkins (PostHog — sales-first, pivot hell, eventual PMF); Jensen Huang (NVIDIA — long arc, public failure, decadal compounding); Paul Graham (Viaweb); Ian Browne (8-year build).

### Cross-path observations
- **Best founders are 22–24+, not 19–20.** The "wunderkind" 19-year-olds often become brand/social influencer monkeys because they lack genuine industry insight. Cluely-style virality ≠ value capture.
- **The path is rarely linear.** Most successful operators did 2–3 different things before their breakout.
- **Domain insight > raw talent.** Hawkins sold for years before PostHog. Davidson sold for years before founding. Junestrand stacked VC internships *before* founding.
- **NI roots are not a blocker** — Browne and Davidson prove the path works from here — but it almost always requires leaving NI for some years.

## 5. Operating Principles

The rules I'm currently using to make decisions. Each is a hypothesis — career-mapper should test these against real data over time.

1. **Don't bank my life on the startup yet.** First paying customer ≠ business. Treat it as a high-EV learning bet, not a destiny. **Finish the degree.**
2. **Use 21–24 to tip-toe down all paths.** Travel, tech jobs, VC internships, further study, building. *Width before depth.*
3. **Elite branding is currency.** Cambridge/Oxford/ICL master's, a top-tier internship name, a known company on the CV — these compound for the rest of my career, especially as a future founder raising capital.
4. **Read history in 3D, not 2D.** The headline ("Altman exited Loopt") is not the lesson (he got rich via network + luck). Decode the mechanics, not the outcome.
5. **Get out of NI for some years.** Almost every role model spent 22–28 in a larger market (London, SF, Singapore, NYC). NI is home and a strong base — not where the compounding happens.
6. **Until ~24: optimize for learning rate.** From 24: optimize for income + optionality. From 27ish: optimize for ownership.
7. **Domain insight > raw talent.** The 22–24 sweet spot for founders exists because that's when domain understanding starts to outweigh youthful naivety. Earn it.
8. **Enjoy being young (PG's law).** Real responsibilities will narrow the lens later. This window is for exploration, character, and worldview — not just résumé building.

## 6. Working Hypothesis — The Next 2–3 Years

My **current best guess** at the path, to be sharpened as career-mapper surfaces real data.

### Year 1 (now → mid-2027): Finish degree + scale or sunset the startup
- Final year of BEng — finish strong, the degree itself matters less than not abandoning it.
- Keep building the startup. Goal: **5–10 paying customers** and a clear answer on whether this is a real company or a great learning project.
- Apply to top master's programmes (Cambridge primary, Oxford/ICL backups) for 2027 entry as **optionality**. Accept only if the startup hasn't crossed a clear "this is real" threshold.
- Apply to elite tech and VC internships for summer 2027 — same logic, optionality.
- Travel. Visit London, SF, Cambridge at minimum. Build the mental map of where I might live next.

### Year 2 (mid-2027 → mid-2028): Width
Branch on what's happened:
- **Branch A** — Cambridge MPhil + summer at a top VC + side-building. Maximally diversified explorer year. Default if startup hasn't crossed the threshold and Cambridge accepts.
- **Branch B** — Go full-time on the startup. Default if I'm at meaningful ARR (e.g. £200k+) with clear growth and can pay myself a real salary.
- **Branch C** — Full-time elite seat (Builder at Anthropic/Stripe/etc. or Hustler at Index/Atomico/EF). Default if neither A nor B's criteria are met but I have a strong offer.

Regardless of branch: **get out of NI**. Live in London, Cambridge, SF, or Singapore.

### Year 3 (mid-2028 → mid-2029): Commit
By 24, with three years of width behind me, pick the path with conviction:
- **If Founder:** the second startup attempt (with real insight + network + a capital cushion).
- **If Builder:** the high-leverage seat earning £200k+ while learning at the frontier.
- **If Hustler:** junior partner track or my own emerging-manager fund.

The point of width 21–24 is so that the choice at 24 is **informed**, not romantic.

## 7. Open Questions

Things I genuinely don't know yet. These are exactly what career-mapper should help answer.

- **Q1 — Salary trajectories.** What's the realistic year-by-year comp curve for each path, 21→30? Real numbers, not vibes.
- **Q2 — Founder survival rates.** Of people who attempted "founder at 21–22" — what fraction reached financial freedom by 30? What did the rest do? Where did they end up?
- **Q3 — Credential vs experience.** What's the right balance between "elite credential" (Cambridge) and "real experience" (top internship + full-time)? Which pays off more and when?
- **Q4 — Geography.** Can I succeed at each path from London, or do I need SF/NYC for some of them? How much does the right city matter at 22 vs 28?
- **Q5 — The off-ramp.** What did successful founders' "Plan B" look like at my age? If the company died, what job did they take, and what did that career end up looking like?
- **Q6 — Trader vs tech.** How does the Merricks-style elite-employee path actually compound vs the tech/VC/founder path over 15 years? Where do they overtake each other (if ever)?
- **Q7 — Alumni networks.** Which specific tech companies and VC firms have the best "alumni-become-successful-founders" track record? Those are the seats to optimize for, full stop.
- **Q8 — Cofounder strategy.** For founders who succeeded on attempt #2: how did they meet their cofounder, and at what stage of their first attempt?

## 8. Tool Brief — `career-mapper`

A personal RAG-based career analysis tool. Grows in value over time as I feed in more data points.

**Status:** v0.1 built 2026-05-16 — see `/career-mapper` (this repo). Sections below describe what was built + the phase roadmap forward.

### Why I'm building it
Career advice is everywhere and mostly noise. I want a system that:
1. Holds high-quality, structured data on role models and job markets — my own curated corpus.
2. Lets me ask my real questions ("Should I do a Cambridge MPhil or go to a top VC for a year?") and get answers grounded in **specific patterns from specific people, with citations**.
3. Surfaces the **outlier vs proven-path distinction explicitly** — so I never pattern-match to a Sam Altman without knowing it.
4. **Grows with me.** Every new role model I research, every new job I learn about, makes the next answer sharper. A compounding personal brain, not a one-off ChatGPT session.

### Data model (v0)

**Person** — the unit of role-model data.
- `name`, `dob_or_age`, `country_of_origin`, `current_country`
- `education[]` — `{ school, degree, start_year, end_year, notes }`
- `career_steps[]` — `{ role, company, start_year, end_year, est_comp, est_age, key_decisions, lessons }`
- `outcomes` — `{ est_net_worth, exit_events[], current_status }`
- `archetype` — `Builder | Hustler | Founder | Outlier`
- `lessons[]` — extracted principles, each with a source citation
- `sources[]` — URLs, interviews, books, podcasts (every claim must trace to a source)

**Company / Job** — the unit of job-market data.
- `company`, `industry`, `hq_country`
- `role`, `level`, `typical_age_range`, `typical_tc_range_by_country`
- `path_in[]` (roles that feed it), `path_out[]` (where it leads next)
- `equity_terms` (if startup)
- `lifestyle_signals` — hours, travel, remote, culture notes

**My profile (`me.json`)** — kept in sync with §1 and §6 of this doc.

### MVP scope (v0.1) — BUILT 2026-05-16
Pivoted from a CLI to a full Next.js app because **the visualization is the killer feature** — text reports invite skim-reading; a spatial map of careers forces every data point into view.

- **Stack.** Next.js 16 (App Router, Turbopack) + React 19 + TypeScript + Tailwind 4.
- **Swimlane timeline.** X = age (15–55), Y = archetype lanes (Investor → Operator → Founder → Hustler → Builder → Student). Each person is a polyline through (age, archetype) space with diagonal segments at every path crossover. Tier styling: Tier 1 solid sky-blue, Tier 2 dashed red, Adjacent dotted purple. "You · 21" green marker shows my current-age line.
- **Person detail panel.** Click any person → bio, education, career steps (with lessons + age), outcomes, sources, confidence indicator, "Ask Claude about this person" button.
- **AskBox.** Free-text question → POST `/api/ask` → Claude Opus 4.7 with corpus + `me.json` context (system block cached) → markdown answer. Cmd/Ctrl+Enter to send.
- **Seed corpus (4 people).** Paul Graham (Tier 1, older era), Max Junestrand (Tier 1, recent — stub-confidence), Ian Browne (Tier 1, NI — stub-confidence), Sam Altman (Tier 2, cautionary).
- **Guardrail system prompt.** Never "you should…". Always cite specific people. Distinguish Tier 1 vs Tier 2 explicitly. Weight recent role models heavier for tactical questions. Every answer ends with a `Confidence:` + `What would sharpen this:` footer.
- **Run.** `cp .env.local.example .env.local` → fill in `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` → `npm install` → `npm run dev` → http://localhost:3000.

### Phase roadmap
- **v0.1 — Seed brain. BUILT 2026-05-16.** Next.js + swimlane timeline + 4 seed people + me.json + `/api/ask` with guardrail prompt + corpus caching.
- **v0.2 — Expand corpus.** On-demand researcher: name a person, Claude (with web search) drafts a `person.json` that I review and accept. Fill in James Hawkins, Gary Davidson, the Merricks-friend benchmark, plus ~20 more across the three tiers.
- **v0.3 — Era ribbon + AI-revolution analog.** Color line segments by era (Pre-PC / PC / Web / Mobile-Cloud / AI). Surface "1995 web ≈ 2024 AI" comparable-moment overlay. Recency-weighting becomes visible on the chart, not just buried in prompts.
- **v0.4 — Pattern dashboard.** Cross-cutting queries: median career timelines, archetype-crossover frequencies, "what did Tier-1 founders do at age X" panels.
- **v0.5 — Decision log.** Every real-life decision I make gets logged with the analysis that informed it. Review yearly.
- **v1.0 — Shared brain.** Possibly opened to a small trusted circle, each with their own `me.json` over a shared role-model corpus.

### Stack — actual choices
- **Runtime.** TypeScript + Node 22 (Next.js 16, React 19, App Router, Turbopack).
- **LLM.** Anthropic Claude Opus 4.7 via official SDK with ephemeral prompt caching on the corpus + me.json system block (read-heavy, perfect cache fit).
- **Storage.** Flat JSON in `/data` (git-versioned, hand-editable).
- **Validation.** zod runtime schema, mirrors `src/lib/types.ts`.
- **Visualization.** D3 scales (`d3-scale`) + React SVG — not D3 DOM manipulation (easier state management, more idiomatic React).
- **Deferred.** SQLite + sqlite-vec (v0.2 when corpus > ~20 people). AI Gateway (only if I want to compare models without rewiring). Deploy to Vercel (whenever I want it available beyond `npm run dev`).
